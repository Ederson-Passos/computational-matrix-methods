\subsubsection*{Item (a) -- Computation of Induced and Entrywise Matrix Norms}

We compute the induced $p$-norms ($p=1,2,\infty$) and the Frobenius norm for a pseudo-randomly generated matrix $A\in\mathbb{R}^{8\times16}$, whose entries are sampled from the discrete set $\{-1,0,1\}$.

The induced norms are defined by the action of $A$ on vectors, whereas the Frobenius norm is an entrywise norm. The formulas used are

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Induced $1$-Norm:}
    \begin{equation}
        \|A\|_1=\max_{1\leq j\leq16}\sum_{i=1}^{8}|a_{ij}|.
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Induced $\infty$-Norm:}
    \begin{equation}
        \|A\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq8}\sum_{j=1}^{16}|a_{ij}|.
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Induced $2$-Norm:}
    \begin{equation}
        \|A\|_2=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(A^TA)}
        =\sigma_{\max}(A).
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Frobenius Norm:}
    \begin{equation}
        \|A\|_F=
        \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{8}\sum_{j=1}^{16}|a_{ij}|^2}
        =\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(A^TA)}.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

The values were computed using the \texttt{numpy.linalg} routines. All numerical results are reported with six significant figures.

The Python script used for the computation is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:python-code}.

\subsubsection*{Item (b) -- Relationship Between Singular Values and the Matrix $2$-Norm}

There is a direct relationship between the singular values of $A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and its induced $2$-norm. According to \cite[Sections 2.3 and 2.4]{golub2013matrix}, the induced $2$-norm is

\begin{equation}
    \|A\|_2
    =\max_{x\neq0}\frac{\|Ax\|_2}{\|x\|_2}
    =\max_{\|x\|_2=1}\|Ax\|_2.
\end{equation}

For any vector $x$,

\begin{equation}
    \|Ax\|_2^2
    =(Ax)^T(Ax)
    =x^T(A^TA)x.
\end{equation}

Let the SVD of $A$ be $A=U\Sigma V^T$. Then

\begin{equation}
    A^TA
    =V(\Sigma^T\Sigma)V^T,
\end{equation}

where the eigenvalues of $A^TA$ are the squared singular values of $A$:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_i(A^TA)=\sigma_i^2.
\end{equation}

Therefore,

\begin{equation}
    \|A\|_2
    =\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(A^TA)}
    =\sqrt{\sigma_{\max}^2}
    =\sigma_{\max}(A).
\end{equation}

Thus, the induced matrix $2$-norm is exactly the largest singular value. For the present matrix, this gives $\|A\|_2=4.50064$.

$A=U\Sigma V^T$. The dimensions and properties of the factors are

\begin{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Item (d) -- Matrix Reconstruction and Norm Comparison ($A_{\mathrm{recon}}$)}

Using the computed factors, the reconstructed matrix is

\begin{equation}
    A_{\mathrm{recon}}=U\Sigma V^T.
\end{equation}

In exact arithmetic, $A_{\mathrm{recon}}=A$. In floating-point arithmetic, the computed factors and matrix products introduce roundoff errors. For IEEE 754 double precision, the unit roundoff is approximately $2^{-53}\approx1.11\times10^{-16}$ \cite[Section 2.7]{golub2013matrix}.

The residual $E=A-A_{\mathrm{recon}}$ was evaluated using the Frobenius and spectral norms.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Residual Error Norms Due to Floating-Point Arithmetic}
\label{tab:residual_norms}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
\toprule
\textbf{Error Norm} & \textbf{Computed Value (6 Significant Figures)} \\
\midrule
$\|A-A_{\mathrm{recon}}\|_F$ & $9.98568\times10^{-15}$ \\
$\|A-A_{\mathrm{recon}}\|_2$ & $6.19227\times10^{-15}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

As expected, $\|E\|_2\leq\|E\|_F$. More generally, for a matrix of rank $r$,

\begin{equation}
    \|E\|_2\leq\|E\|_F\leq\sqrt{r}\|E\|_2
    \cite[Section 2.7]{golub2013matrix}.
\end{equation}

For $r\leq8$,

\begin{equation}
    \frac{\|E\|_F}{\|E\|_2}
    =
    \frac{9.98568\times10^{-15}}
         {6.19227\times10^{-15}}
    \approx1.612605,
\end{equation}

which lies within the theoretical interval $[1,\sqrt{8}]$.

\subsubsection*{Item (e) -- Low-Rank Approximation and Error Norm Analysis}

A rank-$k$ approximation obtained from the truncated SVD is defined by

\begin{equation}
    A_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_i u_i v_i^T.
\end{equation}

Since the assignment does not prescribe a value of $k$, we selected $k=4$, corresponding to half of the maximum possible rank of $A$. The Eckart--Young--Mirsky theorem states that the truncated SVD provides the best rank-$k$ approximation in both the spectral and Frobenius norms \cite[Theorem 2.4.8]{golub2013matrix}.

In particular,

\begin{equation}
    \min_{\operatorname{rank}(B)=k}\|A-B\|_2
    =
    \|A-A_k\|_2
    =
    \sigma_{k+1},
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
    \min_{\operatorname{rank}(B)=k}\|A-B\|_F
    =
    \|A-A_k\|_F.
\end{equation}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Residual Error Norms for the Rank-$4$ Approximation}
\label{tab:rank_k_error}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
\toprule
\textbf{Error Norm} & \textbf{Computed Value ($k=4$, 6 Significant Figures)} \\
\midrule
$\|A-A_4\|_2$ & $3.28669$ \\
$\|A-A_4\|_F$ & $4.54541$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The residual $E_k=A-A_k$ contains the singular values
$\sigma_{k+1},\ldots,\sigma_p$, where $p=\min(m,n)=8$. Consequently,

\begin{equation}
    \|A-A_k\|_2=\sigma_{k+1},
\end{equation}

while

\begin{equation}
    \|A-A_k\|_F
    =
    \sqrt{\sum_{i=k+1}^{p}\sigma_i^2}.
\end{equation}

Thus, the spectral norm is determined by the largest discarded singular value, whereas the Frobenius norm accounts for all discarded singular values \cite[Theorem 2.4.8]{golub2013matrix}. For $k=4$, the computed spectral error agrees with $\sigma_5=3.286685$, while the Frobenius error includes the contributions from $\sigma_5,\ldots,\sigma_8$.

\subsubsection*{Item (a) -- Singular Value Spectrum and Image Complexity}

Using the registration seed, a matrix $A\in\mathbb{R}^{200\times200}$ was generated according to

\begin{equation}
    A=\sin(aX)+\cos(bY),
\end{equation}

where $X$ and $Y$ denote the corresponding grid coordinates.

    \label{fig:matrix_a}
\end{figure}

The singular value decomposition was then used to examine the rank structure of the matrix.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.0\textwidth]{images/svd_spectrum.png}
    \caption{Singular Value Spectrum ($\sigma_i$) of the generated image matrix in logarithmic scale.}
    \label{fig:svd_spectrum}

The matrix has a simple rank structure. The term $\sin(aX)$ depends only on the column coordinate and therefore generates a rank-$1$ matrix, while $\cos(bY)$ depends only on the row coordinate and also generates a rank-$1$ matrix. Hence,

\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{rank}(A)
    \leq
    \operatorname{rank}(\sin(aX))
    +
    \operatorname{rank}(\cos(bY))
    =2.
\end{equation}

The computed singular values confirm this structure. The two dominant values are

\begin{equation}
    \sigma_1=142.031,
    \qquad
    \sigma_2=140.809,
\end{equation}

whereas

\begin{equation}
    \sigma_3=1.26149\times10^{-13}.
\end{equation}

The remaining singular values are likewise at the scale expected from numerical roundoff. Thus, the computed matrix is numerically rank $2$, in agreement with its construction.

The results follow directly from the rank structure established in Item (a). Each component of $A$ is rank $1$, so $\operatorname{rank}(A)\leq2$. Therefore, a rank-$2$ representation is sufficient to reproduce the matrix up to floating-point errors.

For $k=1$, only one of the two dominant singular components is retained. Since $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ have similar magnitudes, the resulting approximation omits a substantial component of the matrix, producing the visible difference shown in Figure~\ref{fig:visual_comparison}. This is consistent with the error $\|A-A_1\|_F=140.809$.

For $k=2$, the two dominant components are retained and the error decreases to approximately $10^{-13}$. This value is consistent with the numerical precision of the computation rather than with a significant structural component omitted from the approximation.

For $k\geq2$, the additional singular values correspond to components whose magnitudes are at the level of numerical roundoff. Consequently, retaining them does not provide a meaningful improvement in the approximation of the analytically rank-$2$ matrix. The small variations in the reported errors for larger $k$ should therefore be interpreted as numerical effects associated with floating-point computation rather than as additional structure in the matrix.
